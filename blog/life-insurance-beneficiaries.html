Life Insurance Beneficiaries: Primary, Contingent, Minors, and Trusts

Learn how life insurance beneficiary designations work, why contingent beneficiaries matter, and what to consider before naming a minor or an estate.

By Daniel Barroso • 7 min read • Educational guide

Watch the 30-Second Overview

Prefer watching? This short companion video provides a quick overview of today's topic. Continue reading below for the complete educational guide.

A beneficiary designation determines who is intended to receive policy proceeds. It can be one of the most important—and most overlooked—parts of a life insurance contract.

Primary and Contingent Beneficiaries

The primary beneficiary is first in line. A contingent beneficiary is a backup if no primary beneficiary can receive the proceeds. Owners may name more than one beneficiary and assign percentages, subject to carrier procedures.

Naming a Minor

A minor generally cannot personally control a large insurance payment. Depending on state law and the amount involved, a court-appointed guardian, custodial arrangement, or trust may be needed. Parents should obtain legal advice before relying on a beneficiary designation as an estate plan.

Naming Your Estate

When the estate receives proceeds, the money may become part of estate administration and may be exposed to estate expenses or creditor claims that might not apply to a direct beneficiary payment. The exact result depends on law and circumstances.

Keep Designations Updated

Review beneficiaries after marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, death, or major estate-plan changes. Do not assume a will automatically overrides the policy’s beneficiary form.

Information a Beneficiary May Need

  • Insurer and policy number, if available
  • Certified death certificate
  • Carrier claim form
  • Proof of identity and tax information
  • Trust or estate documents when applicable

Educational Disclaimer

This article provides general educational information and is not legal, tax, investment, medical, or individualized financial advice. Insurance products, policy features, underwriting, availability, eligibility, costs, and tax treatment vary by carrier, product, state, and individual circumstances. Review the applicable policy contract and consult qualified professionals regarding your situation.
